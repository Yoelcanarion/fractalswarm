- `assumptions[{id, label, value, why, checkedBy}]`, `tokens {coordination, recovery}` and `tokensByModel`, `proof {command, lines[], takeover {command, line}}`.
- Beats: `TREE_KEYFRAMES` (`viz/keyframes.ts`, a name-to-progress record) for the solution stage and `lanes.central.keyframes` for the problem stage.

Visual conventions: components fill their parent (`position: relative`, the parent sets the size); `progress` is a `MotionValue<number>` (0–1; scroll-driven in the stages, time-driven on enter
elsewhere); `still` means the final state with no motion; `DoorGlyph` gets `data = door.id` (`arena`, `live`, `replays`, `director`, `numbers`, `ablation`: **`new-task` no longer exists**, see decision 4).
A visual that draws from `progress` reads it with `useTransform`/`useMotionValueEvent`, never React state per frame.

11. **Stage pacing: the scroll chooses the beat, the clock plays it** (coordinator, Oct 30, 17:06; this replaces the first version, in which the visual was a function of the scroll position through a spring, and the steps were 1.6 to 2.2 screens tall so that the beats had room). Yoel: "las animaciones se ven toscas... hay que scrollear mucho para que carguen las animaciones al completo". The visual is no longer drawn from the scroll:
    - **The step.** The block under the reading line (the middle of the viewport; 0.72 of it on a phone, below the pinned visual) is the current step, with a margin of 0.05 of a screen on either side of a boundary so that a wheel notch at a boundary cannot flip it (`stepAtLine`). The blocks are measured from the DOM: a block is `--w` screens at least and as tall as its card needs.
    - **The beat.** When the step changes one clock plays the visual's progress, and the other channels the step names (`to`), from where they are to the step's targets in the step's own `play` seconds: 0.8 to 1.4 s for a short way, 1.1 to 1.8 s for most, 2.5 s for the step of the four other kills, 2.6 s for the heir (the panels), 3.2 s for the crash-to-restart stretch (the stall counter counts the recorded 101.8 s in that time). A jump over several steps takes the plays added up and divided by 1.82, between 0.6 and 3.6 s. A reader who stops anywhere sees a finished beat at most 3.6 s later; one who scrolls back sees it played back (the step being left gives the time).
    - **The motion** (`tween.ts`): nearly linear, because the visuals ease each of their own elements once over their own stretch of the progress (W7's `span` + smoothstep/ease-out in the tree, the lanes, the panels, the charts), so an ease on the progress as well was an ease on an ease and squeezed every stretch into the middle third of the move (coordinator, Oct 30, from W7's measurement; the first version of this clock was a smoothstep). A ramp of at most 0.2 s (and at most a quarter of the move) up to a constant speed, the constant speed, a ramp down to rest: the ramps are for what has no ease of its own (the stall counter, a bar), so that it does not start and stop with a jolt. Every fifth of the time of a 2 s move covers 0.83 to 1.11 of a fifth of the distance (a smoothstep: 0.52 to 1.4). A retarget in the middle of a move starts from the value *and the speed* of the old one (the speed is held between -1 and 3 times the move's average speed: monotone, no overshoot), so going through several steps is one continuous motion with no stop at each and no stacking, and turning round eases the old speed off first. Not a spring: it ends exactly when it says it will.
    - **Cost.** One animation frame per stage, and only while a move is playing or the page scrolls near it. It reads first (the stage's rect, the only read), then decides the step, then moves the clock. An `IntersectionObserver` (0.5 screen margin) switches a far stage's scroll handler off and stops its clock; the first look at a stage from close by (a page opened half way down, a jump from the rail) puts it on its step with no play and no fade (`data-snap` turns the transitions off for one style flush). No React render during a beat: a step change writes `data-active` on the blocks, `data-current` on the stage and one motion value that the layers read. Measured at the stage's own frame: p50 0.2 to 0.5 ms, max 2.3 ms (Chromium, the wheel at 1800 px/s through each stage).
    - **Cards and layers by state.** A card is at full strength when its step is current and at 0.38 otherwise (a CSS opacity transition, 450 ms, `--dur-step` with `--ease-out`), so the neighbours stay legible and no scroll position leaves the column empty (scanned every 80 px through both stages: at least one card in view everywhere). The layers (the lanes, the stall counter and the parts diagram of the problem stage; the tree, the survivors' panels and the hash strip of the solution stage) are `<Layer name>`s with a visibility per step in the step specs (`layers`), written as `style.opacity` on the step change and faded by a CSS transition with a small hysteresis from the step margin. The survivors' panels play on a channel of their own, `hrw`, 0 to 1 on the heir step and kept at 1 while they fade out (it was a window of the reading line). Transform and opacity only.
    - **Kept.** `?still`, reduced motion (the visual in its final state, the steps in flow), the phone layout, the `#/?at=` targets, the rail, the claim guards. In still mode the parts diagram sits in the card of the last problem step: the lanes and the diagram share one box and used to overlap.
